# Violation Playbooks

Built from `MARKET.md` research on TikTok Seller Center's stated appeal framework: every appeal should address **Root Cause → Immediate Actions Taken → Preventive Measures**. These playbooks are starting points — always tailor to the customer's actual violation notice text (Q3 of intake), and note explicitly to the customer that TikTok's policies can change and this is not a guarantee.

## 1. IP / Counterfeit violation
- **Typical cause:** Listing images, brand names, or logos match a trademark/copyright the seller doesn't hold rights to; or a rights-holder complaint was filed.
- **Evidence to gather:** Proof of authenticity (invoices from authorized suppliers), a licensing agreement if applicable, or documentation the listing has been permanently removed/edited to no longer reference the brand.
- **Common mistake:** Arguing the product is "basically" allowed instead of directly addressing the specific IP claim. Appeals need to show the exact offending content has been removed, not just intent to comply.

## 2. Prohibited / restricted product
- **Typical cause:** Product falls into a category TikTok Shop restricts or bans outright (varies by region/category — check current Seller Center policy for the specific category).
- **Evidence to gather:** Proof the listing has been removed or reclassified; if the seller believes the categorization was wrong, documentation supporting the correct category.
- **Common mistake:** Re-listing the same product without addressing why it was flagged — likely to trigger a second, harder-to-reverse violation.

## 3. Misleading content / listing
- **Typical cause:** Product images, claims, or descriptions don't match the actual item (exaggerated claims, stock photos not representing the real product, misleading pricing/discount claims).
- **Evidence to gather:** Corrected listing screenshots, updated photos of the actual product, and a clear before/after showing the fix.
- **Common mistake:** Only changing the text and not the images, or vice versa — TikTok's review looks at the whole listing.

## 4. Shipping / fulfillment violation
- **Typical cause:** Late shipments, high cancellation rate, or fulfillment SLA breaches.
- **Evidence to gather:** Data/logs showing improved fulfillment times, evidence of the operational fix (new supplier, staffing, processing change), and if applicable, evidence the delay was outside seller control (e.g., carrier-wide disruption).
- **Common mistake:** Blaming the carrier without showing a concrete operational change going forward — "preventive measures" is the weakest part of most seller appeals here.

## 5. Account verification / identity issue
- **Typical cause:** Mismatched business/personal ID documents, incomplete tax/bank info, or a flagged identity-verification step.
- **Evidence to gather:** Clear, unedited copies of the exact documents requested, matching the name/business registered on the account.
- **Common mistake:** Submitting the same rejected document twice without addressing the specific mismatch reason.

## General appeal principles (apply to all categories)
- Address the **specific stated violation**, never a generic apology.
- Structure: what happened (root cause) → what you already fixed (immediate action) → how you'll prevent recurrence (preventive measures) — this mirrors TikTok's own stated framework.
- Be factual and concise; avoid emotional appeals, threats, or arguing the policy itself is wrong.
- Sellers get a limited number of appeal attempts (commonly 2) — the first attempt should be treated as the best/only real shot, not a rough draft.
